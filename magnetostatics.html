Magnetostatics Summary

Core theorems, field quantities, and current distributions across different coordinate systems.

Fundamental Theorems & Relations of Magnetostatics

1. Biot-Savart Law

Relates magnetic field intensity ($H$) to differential current source elements ($I \, d\mathbf{l}$).

2. Ampere's Circuital Law (Integral Form)

$$\oint \mathbf{H} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_{\text{enc}}$$

3. Ampere's Circuital Law (Point Form)

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}$$

4. Non-Existence of Magnetic Monopoles

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0 \implies \oint \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{s} = 0$$

5. Lorentz Force Equation

Combines electric and magnetic forces on a moving charge:$$\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$$

6. Stokes' Theorem (Magnetic Field)

Relates the line integral of $\mathbf{H}$ around a closed path to the surface integral of its curl:$$\oint_C \mathbf{H} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{H}) \cdot d\mathbf{s}$$

7. Divergence Theorem (Magnetic Flux)

Relates the closed surface integral of $\mathbf{B}$ to the volume integral of its divergence:$$\oint_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{s} = \int_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B}) \, dv = 0$$

8. Magnetic Scalar Potential ($\mathbf{V}_m$)

Valid in source-free regions ($\mathbf{J} = 0$):$$\mathbf{H} = -\nabla V_m \quad \text{where} \quad \nabla^2 V_m = 0$$

Core Magnetostatic Quantities, Definitions & Units

Quantity Symbol Definition SI Unit
Magnetic Force $\mathbf{F}_m$ The force exerted on a moving charge or current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Newtons ($\text{N}$)
Magnetic Field Intensity $\mathbf{H}$ A measure of the magnetizing strength produced by current distributions. Amperes per meter ($\text{A/m}$)
Magnetic Flux $\Phi$ The total measure of magnetic field lines passing through a given surface. Webbers ($\text{Wb}$)
Magnetic Flux Density $\mathbf{B}$ The magnetic force experienced per unit current element or magnetic flux per unit area. Teslas ($\text{T}$) or ($\text{Wb/m}^2$)
Magnetic Vector Potential $\mathbf{A}$ A vector field whose curl gives the magnetic flux density ($\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$). Webbers per meter ($\text{Wb/m}$)
Current $I$ The rate of flow of electric charge across a surface. Amperes ($\text{A}$)
Volume Current Density $\mathbf{J}$ The electric current flowing per unit cross-sectional area. Amperes per square meter ($\text{A/m}^2$)
Surface Current Density $\mathbf{K}$ The electric current flowing per unit width along a surface. Amperes per meter ($\text{A/m}$)

Magnetic Force ($\mathbf{F}_m$) Across Geometries & Systems

Moving Point Charge

System: Cartesian / General

$\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$

Current Element / Wire

System: Cartesian / General

$\mathbf{F}_m = I \int (d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B})$

Infinite Parallel Current Wires

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\frac{\mathbf{F}}{L} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d} \mathbf{a}_\rho$

Magnetic Field Intensity ($\mathbf{H}$) Across Geometries & Systems

Infinite Line Current

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\mathbf{H} = \frac{I}{2\pi \rho} \mathbf{a}_\phi$

Infinite Current Sheet

System: Cartesian $(x, y, z)$

$\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{K} \times \mathbf{a}_n)$

Circular Current Loop (Axis)

System: Cylindrical / Axis $z$

$\mathbf{H}_z = \frac{I R^2}{2(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}} \mathbf{a}_z$

Torus / Solenoid (Interior)

System: Cylindrical / Toroidal

$\mathbf{H} = \frac{N I}{2\pi \rho} \mathbf{a}_\phi$ (Torus)  |  $nI$ (Solenoid)

Solid Cylindrical Conductor (Radius $a$)

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

Inside ($\rho < a$): $\mathbf{H} = \frac{I \rho}{2\pi a^2} \mathbf{a}_\phi$  |  Outside ($\rho \ge a$): $\mathbf{H} = \frac{I}{2\pi \rho} \mathbf{a}_\phi$

Magnetic Flux ($\Phi$) & Flux Density ($\mathbf{B}$) Across Systems

Infinite Line Current

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi \rho} \mathbf{a}_\phi$

Infinite Current Sheet

System: Cartesian $(x, y, z)$

$\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{2} (\mathbf{K} \times \mathbf{a}_n)$

Solenoid / Toroid Core

System: Cylindrical

$\mathbf{B} = \mu \frac{N I}{L} \mathbf{a}_z$ (Solenoid)

Magnetic Vector Potential ($\mathbf{A}$) Across Systems

General Volume Current

System: Cartesian / General

$\mathbf{A} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int \frac{\mathbf{J}}{R} dv$

Infinite Line Current

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\mathbf{A} = -\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi} \ln\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_0}\right) \mathbf{a}_z$

Current ($I$) Across Geometries & Systems

Filamentary Current Wire

System: Cartesian / Cylindrical

$I = \text{Discrete Conductor Current } (I)$

Volume Conductor

System: Cartesian $(x, y, z)$

$I = \int \mathbf{J} \cdot d\mathbf{s}$

Surface Current Sheet

System: Cartesian / Cylindrical

$I = \int \mathbf{K} \cdot dl$

Volume Current Density ($\mathbf{J}$) Across Geometries & Systems

Filamentary Line Wire

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\mathbf{J} = I \, \delta(\rho) \mathbf{a}_z$ (Dirac Delta)

Uniform Solid Conductor

System: Cartesian $(x, y, z)$

$\mathbf{J} = \frac{I}{S} \mathbf{a}_n$ ($\text{A/m}^2$)

Solid Cylindrical Conductor (Radius $a$)

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\mathbf{J} = \frac{I}{\pi a^2} \mathbf{a}_z$ (Uniform)

Surface Current Density ($\mathbf{K}$) Across Geometries & Systems

Infinite Current Sheet

System: Cartesian $(x, y, z)$

$\mathbf{K} = \frac{d\mathbf{I}}{dl} = \sigma_c \mathbf{v}$ ($\text{A/m}$)

Cylindrical Shell Conductor (Radius $a$)

System: Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

$\mathbf{K} = \frac{I}{2\pi a} \mathbf{a}_z$ ($\text{A/m}$)

Spherical Shell Conductor (Radius $R$)

System: Spherical $(r, \theta, \phi)$

$\mathbf{K} = \frac{I}{2\pi R \sin\theta} \mathbf{a}_\phi$ ($\text{A/m}$)

Maxwell's Equations (Differential/Point Form & Integral Form)

Law / Name Differential (Point) Form Integral Form Physical Significance
Gauss's Law (Electrostatics) $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} = \rho_v$$ $$\oint_S \mathbf{D} \cdot d\mathbf{s} = Q_{\text{enc}}$$ Electric field lines originate from positive free charges and terminate on negative charges.
Gauss's Law for Magnetism $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$ $$\oint_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{s} = 0$$ Magnetic monopoles do not exist; magnetic flux lines are continuous closed loops.
Faraday's Law (Transformer / Induced EMF) $$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$ $$\oint_C \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{s}$$ Time-varying magnetic flux density produces an electric field (electromotive force).
Ampere-Maxwell Law $$\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$ $$\oint_C \mathbf{H} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S \left(\mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t}\right) \cdot d\mathbf{s}$$ Magnetic fields are produced by conduction currents ($\mathbf{J}$) and time-varying displacement currents ($\frac{\partial \mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$).
