Electromagnetic Field Theory

Your comprehensive summary guide to fields, vectors, coordinate systems, and Maxwell's equations.

Mathematical Foundation: Scalars, Vectors & Coordinate Systems

Electromagnetic Field Theory relies heavily on vector calculus and multi-variable coordinate systems. Below is a mobile-friendly quick-reference summary.

Scalars

Quantities described by magnitude only (e.g., Temperature, Electric Potential $V$, Charge $Q$).

  • No directional component
  • Follows ordinary algebra

Vectors

Quantities with magnitude and direction (e.g., Electric Field $\mathbf{E}$, Magnetic Field $\mathbf{H}$).

  • Expressed with unit vectors
  • Uses dot/cross operations

Standard Coordinate Systems

System Variables Unit Vectors Best Used For
Cartesian $(x, y, z)$ $\mathbf{a}_x, \mathbf{a}_y, \mathbf{a}_z$ Rectangular geometries, planes
Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$ $\mathbf{a}_\rho, \mathbf{a}_\phi, \mathbf{a}_z$ Cables, coaxial wires
Spherical $(r, \theta, \phi)$ $\mathbf{a}_r, \mathbf{a}_\theta, \mathbf{a}_\phi$ Point charges, spherical antennas

Essential Vector Formulas

Dot Product

$$\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = AB \cos\theta$$

Cross Product

$$\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} = AB \sin\theta \, \mathbf{a}_n$$

Gradient

$$\nabla V = \frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\mathbf{a}_x + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y}\mathbf{a}_y + \frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\mathbf{a}_z$$

Divergence

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = \frac{\partial A_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial A_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial A_z}{\partial z}$$

Coordinate Transformation Formulas

Cartesian $\leftrightarrow$ Cylindrical

$\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$
$\phi = \tan^{-1}(y/x)$, $z = z$

$x = \rho \cos\phi$
$y = \rho \sin\phi$, $z = z$

Cartesian $\leftrightarrow$ Spherical

$r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$
$\theta = \cos^{-1}(z/r)$, $\phi = \tan^{-1}(y/x)$

$x = r \sin\theta \cos\phi$
$y = r \sin\theta \sin\phi$, $z = r \cos\theta$

Cylindrical $\leftrightarrow$ Spherical

$r = \sqrt{\rho^2 + z^2}$
$\theta = \tan^{-1}(\rho/z)$, $\phi = \phi$

$\rho = r \sin\theta$
$\phi = \phi$, $z = r \cos\theta$

Vector Operators & Curl Matrices

Cartesian $(x, y, z)$

Div: $\frac{\partial A_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial A_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial A_z}{\partial z}$

Curl Matrix:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{a}_x & \mathbf{a}_y & \mathbf{a}_z \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \end{vmatrix}$$
Cylindrical $(\rho, \phi, z)$

Div: $\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}(\rho A_\rho) + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial A_\phi}{\partial \phi} + \frac{\partial A_z}{\partial z}$

Curl Matrix:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \frac{1}{\rho} \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{a}_\rho & \rho\mathbf{a}_\phi & \mathbf{a}_z \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial \rho} & \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ A_\rho & \rho A_\phi & A_z \end{vmatrix}$$
Spherical $(r, \theta, \phi)$

Div: $\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r^2 A_r) + \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta}(A_\theta \sin\theta)$

Curl Matrix:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta} \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{a}_r & r\mathbf{a}_\theta & r\sin\theta\mathbf{a}_\phi \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial r} & \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} & \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi} \\ A_r & rA_\theta & r\sin\theta A_\phi \end{vmatrix}$$

Advanced Operations & Integral Theorems

Higher-order spatial tracking, vector identities, and multi-variable integration routines applied to physical field problems.

1. The Laplacian ($\nabla^2$)

Divergence of a gradient ($\nabla \cdot \nabla V$). Governs Poisson's and Laplace's equations.

  • Cartesian: $\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial z^2}$
  • Cylindrical: $\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}(\rho\frac{\partial V}{\partial \rho}) + \frac{1}{\rho^2}\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial \phi^2} + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial z^2}$
  • Spherical: $\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r^2\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta}(\sin\theta\frac{\partial V}{\partial \theta})$
2. Field Integrals

Aggregating localized forces across space configurations.

  • Line ($\int \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{l}$): Voltage/work along path contours.
  • Surface ($\iint \mathbf{D} \cdot d\mathbf{s}$): Flux passing a boundary area.
  • Volume ($\iiint \rho \, dv$): Total enclosed charge/sources.
3. Integral Theorems

Connecting macro-scale boundary parameters to micro-scale sources.

  • Divergence Theorem: $\iiint (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) dv = \oint \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{s}$
  • Stokes' Theorem: $\iint (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) \cdot d\mathbf{s} = \oint \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{l}$
4. Wave Equations & Boundaries

Dynamic propagation behavior and material interface conditions.

  • Helmholtz Equation: $\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} - \mu\epsilon \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2} = 0$
  • Boundary Matching: $D_{1n} - D_{2n} = \rho_s$

Scalars and Vectors: Definitions & Properties

1. Scalar Quantity & Field

Definition: A quantity that has only magnitude (e.g., mass, temperature, electric potential). A Scalar Field defines a scalar function distributed over space

Properties: Follows ordinary rules of algebraic addition and multiplication; independent of coordinate direction.

2. Vector Quantity & Field

Definition: A quantity that has both magnitude and a specific direction in space (e.g., velocity, force, electric field intensity $\mathbf{E}$). A Vector Field assigns a vector to every point in space

Properties: Characterized by magnitude and direction, represented using unit vectors, and combines via geometric vector laws.

Vector Algebra Methods & Operations

Scaling of a Vector

Operation: Multiplying a vector $\mathbf{A}$ by a scalar $k$

$k\mathbf{A} = (k A_x)\mathbf{a}_x + (k A_y)\mathbf{a}_y + (k A_z)\mathbf{a}_z$

Addition of Vectors

Operation: Combining two vectors $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ using the parallelogram or head-to-tail rule

$\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B} = (A_x + B_x)\mathbf{a}_x + (A_y + B_y)\mathbf{a}_y + (A_z + B_z)\mathbf{a}_z$

Subtraction of Vectors

Operation: Finding the difference between vector $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$

$\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B} = (A_x - B_x)\mathbf{a}_x + (A_y - B_y)\mathbf{a}_y + (A_z - B_z)\mathbf{a}_z$

Scalar (Dot) Product & Vector (Cross) Product

1. Scalar or Dot Product ($\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}$)

Definition & Formula: Results in a scalar quantity given by $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = AB \cos\theta = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$

Example: For $\mathbf{A} = 2\mathbf{a}_x + 3\mathbf{a}_y$ and $\mathbf{B} = 4\mathbf{a}_x - 5\mathbf{a}_y$, the dot product is $(2)(4) + (3)(-5) + (0)(0) = 8 - 15 = -7$

2. Vector or Cross Product ($\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}$)

Definition & Formula: Results in a vector perpendicular to both, given by $\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} = AB \sin\theta \, \mathbf{a}_n$ or evaluated via determinant.

Example: For $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{a}_x + 2\mathbf{a}_y$ and $\mathbf{B} = 3\mathbf{a}_z$, $\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} = (\mathbf{a}_x \times 3\mathbf{a}_z) + (2\mathbf{a}_y \times 3\mathbf{a}_z) = -3\mathbf{a}_y + 6\mathbf{a}_x$

Cross Products of Cartesian Unit Vectors

Self Cross Products

Parallel vectors ($\theta = 0^\circ$):

$\mathbf{a}_x \times \mathbf{a}_x = 0$

$\mathbf{a}_y \times \mathbf{a}_y = 0$

$\mathbf{a}_z \times \mathbf{a}_z = 0$

Cyclic Cross Products

Right-hand screw progression:

$\mathbf{a}_x \times \mathbf{a}_y = \mathbf{a}_z$

$\mathbf{a}_y \times \mathbf{a}_z = \mathbf{a}_x$

$\mathbf{a}_z \times \mathbf{a}_x = \mathbf{a}_y$

Anti-Cyclic Cross Products

Reversed direction:

$\mathbf{a}_y \times \mathbf{a}_x = -\mathbf{a}_z$

$\mathbf{a}_z \times \mathbf{a}_y = -\mathbf{a}_x$

$\mathbf{a}_x \times \mathbf{a}_z = -\mathbf{a}_y$

Basic Laws of Vector Addition, Subtraction, and Scalar Multiplication

Vector Addition Laws

Commutative Law: $\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B} + \mathbf{A}$

Associative Law: $(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B}) + \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A} + (\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{C})$

Scalar Multiplication Laws

Distributive Law (Scalar): $(r + s)\mathbf{A} = r\mathbf{A} + s\mathbf{A}$

Distributive Law (Vector): $r(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B}) = r\mathbf{A} + r\mathbf{B}$

Associative & Identity Laws

Associative (Scalars): $r(s\mathbf{A}) = (rs)\mathbf{A}$

Identity Law: $1\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}$

Laws of Vector Subtraction

Vector Subtraction Definition

Subtraction of a vector is defined in terms of addition with the negative of the vector: $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{A} + (-\mathbf{B})$

Non-Commutative Property

Vector subtraction is not commutative, as reversing the order yields the negative result: $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B} = -(\mathbf{B} - \mathbf{A})$

Position, Distance, Differential Elements & Coordinate Ranges

Cartesian Coordinates $(x, y, z)$

Variable Ranges:
$-\infty < x < \infty$,
$-\infty < y < \infty$,
$-\infty < z < \infty$

Constant Planes:
$x = \text{const}$ (yz-plane),
$y = \text{const}$ (xz-plane),
$z = \text{const}$ (xy-plane)

Position Vector & Distance:
$\mathbf{r} = x\mathbf{a}_x + y\mathbf{a}_y + z\mathbf{a}_z$,
$R = \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2 + \Delta z^2}$

Differential Length & Volume:
$d\mathbf{l} = dx\mathbf{a}_x + dy\mathbf{a}_y + dz\mathbf{a}_z$,
$dv = dx\,dy\,dz$

Cylindrical Coordinates
$(\rho, \phi, z)$

Variable Ranges:
$0 \le \rho < \infty$,
$0 \le \phi < 2\pi$,
$-\infty < z < \infty$

Constant Surfaces:
$\rho = \text{const}$ (cylinder),
$\phi = \text{const}$ (half-plane),
$z = \text{const}$ (plane)

Position Vector & Distance:
$\mathbf{r} = \rho\mathbf{a}_\rho + z\mathbf{a}_z$,
$R = \sqrt{\rho_1^2 + \rho_2^2 - 2\rho_1\rho_2\cos(\phi_2-\phi_1) + (z_2-z_1)^2}$

Differential Length & Volume:
$d\mathbf{l} = d\rho\mathbf{a}_\rho + \rho d\phi\mathbf{a}_\phi + dz\mathbf{a}_z$,
$dv = \rho\,d\rho\,d\phi\,dz$

Spherical Coordinates
$(r, \theta, \phi)$

Variable Ranges:
$0 \le r < \infty$,
$0 \le \theta \le \pi$,
$0 \le \phi < 2\pi$

Constant Surfaces:
$r = \text{const}$ (sphere),
$\theta = \text{const}$ (cone),
$\phi = \text{const}$ (half-plane)

Position Vector & Distance:
$\mathbf{r} = r\mathbf{a}_r$,
$R = \sqrt{r_1^2 + r_2^2 - 2r_1r_2[\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2 + \sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2\cos(\phi_2-\phi_1)]}$

Differential Length & Volume:
$d\mathbf{l} = dr\mathbf{a}_r + r\,d\theta\mathbf{a}_\theta + r\sin\theta\,d\phi\mathbf{a}_\phi$,
$dv = r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$

Properties and Applications of Scalar (Dot) Product

Properties of Scalar Product

1. Commutative Law: $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{A}$

2. Distributive Law: $\mathbf{A} \cdot (\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} + \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{C}$

3. Scalar Multiplication: $(k\mathbf{A}) \cdot \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{A} \cdot (k\mathbf{B}) = k(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B})$

4. Orthogonality: $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$ if $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are perpendicular ($\theta = 90^\circ$)

5. Parallel Vectors: $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = AB$ if vectors are parallel ($\theta = 0^\circ$)

6. Anti-Parallel Vectors: $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = -AB$ if vectors are anti-parallel ($\theta = 180^\circ$)

7. Self-Product (Magnitude squared): $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{A} = A^2 \implies A = \sqrt{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{A}}$

8. Unit Vector Dot Products: $\mathbf{a}_x \cdot \mathbf{a}_x = \mathbf{a}_y \cdot \mathbf{a}_y = \mathbf{a}_z \cdot \mathbf{a}_z = 1$ and cross-axes products equal $0$

Applications of Scalar Product

Finding Angle Between Vectors: $\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{AB}$

Component Projection: Projection of vector $\mathbf{A}$ in the direction of vector $\mathbf{B}$ is given by $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{a}_b$

Work Done Calculation: Work done is expressed as the dot product of force and displacement vectors ($W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d}$)
