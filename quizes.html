Topic: Coulomb's Law & Electrostatic Force (EE / ECE)

GATE | EE / ECE
Two point charges $Q_1 = 2\,\mu\text{C}$ and $Q_2 = -4\,\mu\text{C}$ are placed at coordinates $(0, 0, 0)$ m and $(0, 1, 0)$ m respectively in free space. The electrostatic force vector acting on $Q_2$ is:
  • A) $-\mathbf{a}_y \, 71.9$ mN
  • B) $+\mathbf{a}_y \, 71.9$ mN
  • C) $-\mathbf{a}_x \, 35.8$ mN
  • D) $+\mathbf{a}_z \, 71.9$ mN
Answer: A
Explanation: Using Coulomb's Law, $\mathbf{F}_{12} = \frac{Q_1 Q_2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 |\mathbf{R}_{12}|^3} \mathbf{R}_{12}$. The position vector from $Q_1$ to $Q_2$ is $\mathbf{R}_{12} = \mathbf{a}_y$ m and $|\mathbf{R}_{12}| = 1$ m. Substituting the values yields a force of $-71.9\,\mathbf{a}_y$ mN acting on $Q_2$.
ESE (IES) | EE
If the relative permittivity ($\varepsilon_r$) of the medium between two point charges increases from 1 to 4, the electrostatic force between them will:
  • A) Increase by a factor of 4
  • B) Decrease by a factor of 4
  • C) Remain unchanged
  • D) Decrease by a factor of 2
Answer: B
Explanation: Electrostatic force is inversely proportional to the permittivity of the medium: $F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$. When $\varepsilon_r$ increases from 1 to 4, the force decreases by a factor of 4.
State PSU (AE/AEE) & YCT | ECE
Three identical point charges, each of magnitude $q$, are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side length $a$. The magnitude of the electrostatic force experienced by any one charge is:
  • A) $\frac{q^2\sqrt{3}}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 a^2}$
  • B) $\frac{\sqrt{3}q^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 a^2}$
  • C) $\frac{q^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 a^2\sqrt{3}}$
  • D) $\frac{3q^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 a^2}$
Answer: B
Explanation: The force due to each of the other two charges is $F_0 = \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a^2}$. Since they act at an angle of $60^\circ$, the resultant force is $F = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + 2F_0^2 \cos(60^\circ)} = \sqrt{3}F_0 = \frac{\sqrt{3}q^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 a^2}$.
